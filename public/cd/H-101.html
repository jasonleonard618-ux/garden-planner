GENERAL NOTES

  1. Loads: one full-depth box of honey weighs over 60 lb; a colony ready for winter about 125 lb. Summer stacks with supers weigh more. Size the stand for 300 lb per hive. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93) (weights); 300 lb (practical choice)
  2. 18" to the rail limits skunk and rodent damage. Source: MU Extension G7600, Beekeeping Tips for Beginners In snow, a top entrance keeps air moving if the lower one is buried. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  3. Sun: Penn State favors open sun (shaded colonies are more defensive); the CAES 2014 sheet says not to keep hives in direct sun. Sources differ. We suggest morning sun with light afternoon shade. (practical choice)
  4. Residential lots: one or two hives. Source: CAES, Hive Location, Ownership and Management in CT (2014). Penn State: no more than four on 1/4 acre or less.
  5. Before bees arrive: check town zoning and HOA rules, and register with the State Entomologist, free, then every year by October 1. Source: CAES Apiary Registration (CGS 22-89)
  6. Example site in view 5; your sheet uses the spot you choose in the hive site check.

KEYNOTES

  1. 14×4 leg, ground-contact pressure-treated or cedar, set on its paver, not buried. (practical choice)
  2. 22×6 rail, front and back, 6'-0", outside faces of legs. Hives bear on both rails.
  3. 32×6 block between rails at each leg pair and at center, two 3" screws each end.
  4. 4Two 1/2" × 6" carriage bolts per leg. Hot-dip galvanized or stainless in treated wood. Source: IRC R317.3
  5. 5Back legs and rail 1/2" higher so the hive tips forward and rain runs out the entrance. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  6. 612" × 12" × 2" concrete paver on 4" compacted gravel, level side to side. Keeps legs dry. (practical choice)
  7. 7Hive, by beekeeper. A stand keeps the floor dry and grass out of the entrance. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  8. 8Entrance faces south or east for sun through the day. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  9. 9Work space between hives to set boxes on while inspecting. (practical choice)
  10. 10Mow around the stand and keep it clear of debris. Source: CAES, Hive Location, Ownership and Management in CT (2014)
  11. 11Flyway barrier at least 6 ft high (solid fence, hedge or hay bales) between hives and paths or beds, so bees fly up over people. Source: CAES, Hive Location, Ownership and Management in CT (2014)
  12. 12Water source 25 to 50 ft from the hives, filled by March 15. Source: CAES, Hive Location, Ownership and Management in CT (2014). Keep it filled to October 31. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  13. 13Property line. Hives within 20 ft need a solid fence or hedge 5 ft or taller between hives and the line. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
  14. 14Evergreens, a hill or a building on the windward side. Source: Penn State / MAAREC, Beekeeping Basics (AGRS-93)
