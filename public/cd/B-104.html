GENERAL NOTES

  1. Set the tank on its blocks before filling. A filled 2×8 tank holds about 1 cu yd of mix and is roughly a ton when wet; it cannot be moved. Check structure before placing on a deck or roof. (practical choice) (weight estimate)
  2. Why a stock tank: no carpentry, 24" of root depth, and a 28" working height. Trade-offs: costs more per square foot than a wood bed, takes more soil, and the round ends lose some planting space.
  3. Zinc: Extension sources call galvanized beds generally safe for food crops; zinc can leach more when soil pH is below 6. Keep pH 6.0 to 7.0, test every few years, and skip blueberries and acid amendments. Source: UMD HGIC, Ask Extension #919239 (2025); Ask Extension #828661 (2023)
  4. Heat: sources disagree. One Extension answer says the tank may get very hot; others say moist soil evens it out. No trial found. Keep the soil from drying out (drip line). Source: Ask Extension #872397, #895982
  5. Life span: steel life depends on thickness, coating, soil pH and moisture. The 35 to 50 year figure applies to 14 ga steel (drainage pipe research); tanks are about 21 ga, thinner, and no source gives a life for them. Makers warrant 1 year. Source: Virginia Tech SPES-425 (2022)
  6. Wear gloves when drilling; drilled edges are sharp.

KEYNOTES

  1. 1New galvanized steel stock tank, round-end (oval), 2'×8'×2' nominal. Do not use old or rusty tanks; a new tank is less likely to release metals. Source: Ask Extension #828661 (2023)
  2. 2Factory rolled top rim. Leave as built, so there is no cut edge at the top.
  3. 3Factory drain plug: remove and leave open. It sits about 1" up the end wall, so it cannot drain the floor by itself.
  4. 4Drill 1/2" drain holes in the floor on the centerline, 12" O.C. (7 holes). One factory hole is not enough. Source: Ask Extension #874870 (2024) Hole size and spacing (practical choice)
  5. 5Deburr each hole and touch up the bare edge with zinc-rich cold-galvanizing spray. (practical choice)
  6. 64×8×16 solid concrete blocks laid flat in two rails under the long sides, leaving the center strip and holes open. A block base keeps drain holes open. Source: OSU Extension Master Gardener, Ask Extension #610289 (2020)
  7. 7Strip sod, level the spot, 2" compacted stone dust under blocks. (practical choice)
  8. 8Raised-bed soil mix, one mix for the full depth, filled to 2" below the rim. Water in and top up after settling. No gravel or rock layer at the bottom; layers hold water up instead of draining it. Source: UMD Extension (2023), UW-Madison XHT1278 (2021), L. Chalker-Scott, WSU
  9. 9Optional plastic liner on the side walls only, not the floor, for extra caution about zinc. A floor liner can block drainage. Source: OSU Extension Master Gardener, Ask Extension #610289 (2020)
  10. 10Drip emitter tubing, 2 runs per tank, 12" emitter spacing. See D-101. Source: Clemson HGIC 1678
  11. 12Alternate fill to save soil: untreated logs or branches in the bottom, gaps packed with the same mix, at least 12" of mix above. No landscape fabric between. Source: Ask Extension #874870 (2024) (filler), UW-Madison XHT1278 (2021) (12 to 18" depth for tomatoes, peppers, squash) Wood settles as it rots; top up yearly.
  12. 13Tank floor, galvanized steel.
